# Product Brief: Production Tracking System (PTS)

## Executive Summary

A mid-sized engineering and production company in Agder designs and delivers marine products for its customers. Production is carried out both internally and through external suppliers, such as machining and welding workshops.

The company needs a better way to track production jobs as they move from engineering and purchasing, through supplier production, inspection, and delivery, to internal assembly. Today, information is spread across Excel files, emails, and individual follow-ups, making it difficult to maintain a clear and reliable overview.

The Production Tracking System (PTS) will provide an internal web application for tracking this information. External suppliers exchange updates through controlled Excel files rather than accessing the company's internal systems directly, since a level of classified documentation adds another layer of complexity.

**All data in this project is fictional.** The case is based on a real type of company, but no real company data, drawings, project names, or supplier information is stored in the repository.

## The Problem

The company may have many production jobs running at the same time, often involving several suppliers and internal departments.

A typical job moves through:

**Engineering → Supplier → Production → Inspection → Delivery → Internal Assembly**

At each stage, employees need to know the status, expected delivery date, inspection status, and whether any problems have occurred — for example, wrong measurements on a drawing, which must be captured by engineering and result in a new drawing revision.

When this information is managed through separate Excel files, emails, and manual follow-ups, it becomes difficult to know which information is current and accurate. Issues such as drawing problems, missing materials, production delays, or revision mismatches can also be hard to identify and follow up on.

The company also operates in a controlled IT environment, meaning external suppliers should not have direct access to internal systems or databases.

## The Solution

PTS gives the internal user one overview of production jobs and their current stage, and a controlled way to exchange updates with suppliers through Excel.

### What a job is

A **job** is one drawing that a supplier must produce. A production order can have well over a hundred jobs, grouped by room, because suppliers produce one room at a time.

### What the system shows per job

- Job ID, generated by PTS
- Room, and an internal area (for example a deck)
- Drawing number and current revision
- Number of sheets
- Supplier
- The date each production stage was completed, and the **active stage**
- Whether the job is stopped, and the reason
- Due date and the supplier's expected delivery date
- Open issues and warnings (delay, revision mismatch, ready for inspection)

### The internal user's workflow

1. **Create jobs** in PTS, one at a time or many at once by importing a **job package**, an Excel file that lists the drawings to be produced.
2. **Download an Excel file** for one supplier, containing that supplier's open jobs sorted by room.
3. **Send the file to the supplier** by email or through a shared folder. This transfer happens outside PTS and is done manually.
4. **Receive the updated file** from the supplier (email or shared folder) and **upload it** to PTS.
5. **Review the import.** PTS validates every row and shows, per job, what changed compared to the database, which rows were rejected and why, and which rows carry warnings.
6. **Approve or reject the whole import.** Only after approval is the database updated with the file's contents.
7. **Follow up** from the dashboard: jobs that are stopped, ready for inspection, delayed, or affected by a new drawing revision.
8. **Record phone calls.** When a supplier phones in a change, the user records it as a **manual update** of the supplier-reported fields. Every manual update is stored in the job's history with date, user, old and new value.
9. **Register delivery.** When the company has received a job, the user signs the delivery with a date, and the job closes.

A job **closes** when its delivery is registered. The user can also close a job by hand, for example when an order is cancelled, and reopen it. Only open jobs are included in supplier files.

**Language:** everything users and suppliers see is in Norwegian, including the Excel file's column names, values and instructions. This brief, the PRD and the code are in English. The Norwegian labels are listed in the PRD (§3.1).

### Production stages

A job moves through six stages in a fixed order. The supplier enters a date when a stage is completed, and an empty cell means the stage is not completed.

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Material | The material is in place. The supplier buys it. |
| Welded | Welding is finished. |
| Inspected | The job has been inspected after welding. |
| Blasted | Surface treatment, first step. |
| Painted | Surface treatment, second step. |
| Final inspection | The job has passed the final inspection. |

- The **active stage** is calculated: it is the first stage without a date. It is never typed in.
- After the final inspection, the supplier prepares the job for shipping. This is not a separate stage with its own date. The job stays in *Preparation and shipping* until the company registers **Delivered**.
- **Delivered** is signed by the company with a date when the job is received. It is not part of the supplier file.
- A job that is **stopped** can be at any stage. The supplier sets *Stopped* and must give a reason.
- A date on a stage while an earlier stage has no date is not rejected. It gives a warning.

### Drawing revisions

Drawings themselves live outside PTS. When a new drawing revision is approved for production, the internal user places it in the shared folder with the supplier and informs the project manager and job owner by email, as today. In addition, the user **updates the current revision on the job in PTS manually**. PTS then marks the job as *"new revision — send updated Excel to supplier"*, and flags any later import that still reports the old revision. The stage dates stay as they are.

The new revision is marked clearly in three places:

- **Dashboard:** the job shows a visible *New revision* badge until the supplier has been sent an updated file.
- **Excel file:** in the next export to the supplier, the Revision cell of the job is highlighted and the Revision note column says `NEW REVISION`, so the supplier sees which drawings have changed.
- **Clearing the mark:** the dashboard badge is removed automatically when a new export for that supplier, containing the job, has been downloaded.

PTS stores only the **current** revision of each job, not a revision history. The import log still shows which revision each supplier file reported.

## The Excel File

Each export is a fixed template with locked and editable columns. The column names and values are shown in English here, and the file itself uses Norwegian labels.

### Locked columns (visible to the supplier, not to be changed)

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| Export ID | Unique per exported file |
| Export date | Date the file was generated |
| Job ID | |
| Room | The file is sorted by room |
| Drawing number | |
| Revision | Current revision at the time of export; highlighted when it changed since the previous export to this supplier |
| Revision note | `NEW REVISION` when the revision changed since the previous export to this supplier, otherwise empty `[ASSUMPTION]` |
| Number of sheets | |
| Supplier | |
| Due date | The company's required date, set per job |

### Editable columns (filled in by the supplier)

| Column | Allowed values |
|---|---|
| Material, Welded, Inspected, Blasted, Painted, Final inspection | A valid date, or empty (real Excel date, or text as `dd.mm.yyyy` or `yyyy-mm-dd`) |
| Stopped | `Yes` or `No` |
| Stop reason | `Drawing issue`, `Missing material`, `Check requested`, `Other` (required when Stopped is `Yes`) |
| Expected delivery date | A valid date, or empty |
| Comment / issue description | Free text (required when Stop reason is `Other`) |

**Dropdowns:** Stopped and Stop reason are dropdown lists in the Excel file (Excel data validation), so the supplier picks from the allowed values instead of typing them. The stage columns and the expected delivery date use Excel data validation for dates. Because suppliers can still paste values or use other spreadsheet programs, PTS validates every row on import regardless of the dropdowns.

The area, the delivered date and internal-only information, such as inspection results, are not included in the file.

## Validation Rules

Every uploaded file is first checked as a whole, then every row is checked against the rules below.

### File rejected (nothing is imported and the reason is shown)

- **F1.** The file is not an `.xlsx` file, or cannot be read.
- **F2.** One or more required columns from the template are missing or renamed.
- **F3.** The Export ID is missing or was not generated by PTS.
- **F4.** The file contains no job rows.
- **F5.** The Export ID belongs to an earlier export than the latest export for that supplier. Once several pending imports are allowed, such a file is accepted with warning 9 instead.

### Row rejected (the row is not imported and the reason is shown)

1. Stopped is not `Yes`, `No` or empty, or the stop reason is not an allowed value.
2. Stopped is `Yes` without a stop reason, or the reason is `Other` without a comment.
3. *Retired. The number is not reused.*
4. *Retired. The number is not reused.*
5. A stage date or the expected delivery date is not a valid date.
6. Job ID does not exist in PTS, or belongs to a different supplier.
7. **Duplicate Job ID:** the same Job ID appears more than once in the file. All rows with that Job ID are rejected.

### Warning (the row is imported on approval, but flagged for the user)

8. **Revision mismatch:** the revision in the file differs from the job's current revision in PTS, meaning the supplier may be working from an outdated drawing.
9. **Outdated file:** a newer export exists for the same supplier.
10. **Unexpected change:** a locked column has been changed by the supplier.
11. **Delay:** the expected delivery date is later than the due date.
12. **Stage out of order, or date removed:** a stage has a date while an earlier stage has none, or the file removes a stage date the job already has.
13. **Missing job:** an open job that was included in the export is missing from the uploaded file. The job is left unchanged.
14. **Job changed by another import:** after the export, another approved import changed a field of this job, and the file's value for that field differs from the job's current value.
15. **Conflict with manual update:** after the export, the user changed a field of this job by a manual update, and the file's value for that field differs from the job's current value.

### Import rules

- **Nothing is written to the database without approval.**
- An import is approved or rejected **as a whole**. Rejected rows are never imported, even when the import is approved.
- **Only cells the supplier changed are applied.** A cell whose value equals the value in the export is never applied, so an old, untouched value cannot undo newer information, such as a manual update.
- **When a field changed in PTS after the export and the file disagrees (warnings 14 and 15):**
  - If the supplier did not touch the cell, PTS keeps its own value and shows the warning as information.
  - If the supplier changed the cell, the user **chooses per field** between the file's value and the PTS value. No choice is preselected, and the import cannot be approved until every such field has a choice.
- **Core:** a supplier has one pending import at a time, and only a file from the supplier's latest export is accepted (F5). Conflicts then cannot occur.
- **Extension:** several pending imports are allowed, also for the same supplier. Every pending import is always shown against the current database, so the user never approves against stale values.
- Every approved or rejected import is logged with date, file, user, result and the choice made for each conflict.

## Who This Serves

**Primary user in v1:**
- **Production planner** (one internal role). Creates jobs, exports and imports Excel files, approves imports, registers new drawing revisions and deliveries, and follows up from the dashboard.

In practice several internal employees may use PTS, but v1 has **one internal user** to keep the system simple. **No login** in v1; the system records a fixed user name on every approval, rejection, manual update and delivery so the import log and job history remain traceable.

**Indirect user:**
- **External suppliers** never access PTS. They only receive and return the Excel file.

**Later versions:** project and engineering personnel, and quality and inspection personnel, as separate roles.

Success means the internal user can quickly answer a basic question: *"Where is this job, and is there anything we need to act on?"*

## Success Criteria

All criteria below can be checked by testing v1.

1. A job can be created and is shown on the dashboard with all fields listed under "What the system shows per job".
2. An Excel file can be downloaded for one supplier, containing only that supplier's open jobs sorted by room, with a unique Export ID and the current revision of each job.
3. An uploaded file is validated, and every rejected row is shown with an understandable error message that names the rule that was broken.
4. Before approval, the user can see exactly what changed per job compared to the database.
5. **No import ever changes the database before it is approved** — verified by tests.
6. A file reporting an older revision than the job's current revision is flagged as a revision mismatch.
7. A job whose expected delivery date is later than its due date is flagged as delayed on the dashboard.
8. *(Extension)* When two pending imports contain the same job and one is approved, the other import shows the updated values, and a field that would overwrite them is shown as a conflict the user decides per field.
9. From the dashboard alone, the user can find all jobs that are stopped, ready for inspection, delayed, or affected by a new revision.
10. In an exported file, Stopped and Stop reason are dropdown lists containing only the allowed values, and the stage columns accept only dates.
11. A job with a new revision is marked in the dashboard and in the next export to its supplier, and the dashboard mark disappears once that export has been downloaded.
12. A file that is not a valid PTS export (wrong file type, missing columns, unknown Export ID or no rows) is rejected as a whole with a clear message.

## Scope

### In v1

- Job register: create, view and edit jobs, register a new drawing revision manually, register deliveries, and (as an extension) create many jobs from a job package, record manual updates with a job history, and close and reopen jobs by hand
- Excel export per supplier, using the fixed template with dropdown lists and new revisions marked
- Excel import with file checks, row validation, change view, warnings and rejected rows
- Approval or rejection of a whole import, with an import log, and as an extension several pending imports and a choice per conflict field
- Dashboard with stages, stopped jobs, delays, jobs ready for inspection and a clear new-revision mark
- Seed data and example Excel files with fictional data, so the app can be run and tried locally
- A Norwegian user interface and supplier file

The PRD divides v1 into **Core** (create job → export → import → change view → approve → dashboard), which must be finished and stable first, starting with a Core minimum of 20 requirements, and **Extension** (conflict handling, job package import, closing and reopening by hand, and supplier management).

### Out of scope for v1

- Recording inspection results, internal assembly and final approval. Only the dates the supplier completes *Inspected* and *Final inspection* are tracked
- Multiple roles and login
- Revision history
- Approving or rejecting individual rows in an import
- Sending email or integrating with shared folders — file transfer is manual
- Replacing the company's ERP, PDM, PLM, MES, or quality systems
- Storing engineering drawings as the authoritative source
- Automatically approving technical or quality decisions
- Giving suppliers direct access to the internal network
- Becoming a complete warehouse, logistics, or financial system

PTS is an information and tracking tool that helps the internal user maintain an accurate overview and identify problems early — not a system of record for engineering or finance.

## Vision

The long-term vision is a practical production tracking system that follows a job through the company's wider production process:

**Engineering → Supplier Production → Inspection → Delivery → Internal Assembly → Final Approval**

Possible later steps:

- Several internal users with **Windows / Microsoft Entra ID sign-in**, and separate roles for project, engineering, production and quality personnel
- Recording inspection results, and internal assembly
- Automatic notification of project manager and job owner when a drawing revision changes
- Approving individual rows in an import
- Measuring business effects, such as less time spent on manual follow-up with suppliers and fewer errors caused by outdated drawing revisions, once baseline numbers are available

The system should remain simple and focused: helping people make better decisions by providing the right production information at the right time, without duplicating the company's existing ERP, PDM, or quality systems.
